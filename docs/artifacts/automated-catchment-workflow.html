Scope. extent_pct is observed surface water — water availability as seen from above — not rainfall and not climate. Regulation, extraction, diversion and land-use change move extent independently of rainfall. The rainfall comparison below tests exactly this: whether an aseasonal or marginal extent regime reflects the local climate or something else acting on the water.

Per-catchment detail — water extent, events, and rainfall context

Each panel: wet episodes (blue bars, height = magnitude) and low-extent spells (amber) on the 40-year timeline, then the monthly climatology of rainfall (green bars/line) alongside extent (blue line) so the two shapes can be compared directly. Low-extent spells mean below this record's own typical month, not literal dryness — the base rate is reported so a long spell on a catchment that sits below baseline a third of the time isn't misread as drought.

What the workflow can report, per catchment

Routing = hydroseason.analyze_catchment(); events = extract_water_events(), noise-relative hysteresis thresholds (enter 3σ, exit 1σ of the record's own AR(1)-corrected month-to-month noise), so a recession wobble does not split one flood into several, and a 40-year record does not accrue spurious single-month "floods" from chance alone. Rainfall = compare_extent_and_rainfall_regimes(), SILO gridded monthly rainfall (_rainfall.py, lazy per-AOI S3 reads, no national download) assessed with the identical regime function used on extent — rainfall is compared against the extent verdict, never merged into it. Boundaries carry boundary_basis = detected_per_year or imposed_fixed_window, so an assumption the workflow made is never mistaken for a feature it found. All figures 30 m extent, 1986-05 → 2026-06.
