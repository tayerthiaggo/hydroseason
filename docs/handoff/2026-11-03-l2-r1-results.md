## Summary

**Both tests fail. Nothing changes in the library, and neither result makes a candidate.**

- **L2 fails rules 1 and 2 in both arms.**
  - Comparing the months after the trough boundary as well makes `point` only slightly more reliable. The `point` miss share falls from 30.2% to 27.7% in arm S, and from 24.3% to 22.9% in arm I. The plan required a fall of at least 5 percentage points.
  - `point` stays less reliable than `interval`: 27.7% against 21.6% (arm S), and 22.9% against 15.9% (arm I).
  - Rules 3 and 4 hold: no records are lost and no date moves.
- **R1 fails.** The amplitude-threshold rise does not date the dry-to-wet transition more accurately than the trough boundary in both arms and both shapes.
  - On recession (`pulse`) seasons it is right more often: +25.7 points in arm S, which passes rule 1. In arm I the gain is +4.7 points, just short of the +5 required.
  - On smooth (`sine`) seasons it is right less often: −12.0 points (arm S) and −19.8 points (arm I). Rule 2 fails in both arms.
  - Coverage passes: 100.0% (arm S) and 99.8% (arm I) of pairs have a rise.
- **The pre-registered readings apply.** For L2, the default is no change. For R1, the plan reads a fail as "this rise rule gives no accuracy gain over the trough boundary". The gain on recession seasons in arm S is real, but it does not carry over to smooth seasons. A rise anchor gets no support here.

Exploratory checks on the same runs (not pre-registered) explain L2 and found one inconsistency in the library's output. Details are under "Exploratory findings":
- most `point` dates that still miss are a single month that really reads lowest in the data, more than a floor below the true month;
- in 13% of arm I's `point` trough cycles, the `point` label belongs to an earlier, cloudy month, not to the reported `trough_date`.

## Exploratory findings (not pre-registered)

These were looked at after scoring. They inform the next decisions and decide nothing.

**1. The `point` dates that still miss are single months that read lowest in the data.** Take the `two_sided` `point` misses whose label month is the boundary itself (509 in arm S, 653 in arm I).
- The true month was observed (usable) in 98% (arm S) and 96% (arm I) of them.
- Where it was observed, it reads more than one detectability floor above the boundary in 55–61% (arm S) and 75% (arm I) of them, with the floor taken from the cycle's `amplitude_to_floor_ratio`. A one-floor comparison cannot merge those months, on either side.
- Most are early: 90% in arm S and 75% in arm I.

With L1, this means neither a wider tolerance (L1) nor a two-sided comparison (L2) fixes `point`. In these records, the lowest month often stands out from the true trough month by more than a floor.

**2. The `point` label can belong to a different month than `trough_date`.** In arm I, 617 of 4,724 `point` trough cycles (13.1%) have a one-month trough interval that is not the boundary month. In arm S, 64 of 2,981 (2.1%) do. In every case the label's month comes before the boundary. In 568 of 617 (arm I) and 47 of 64 (arm S), that month is more than 20% invalid.
- The cause, in the cloudy cases: the trough label compares every candidate month, including cloudy ones. The trough selection then screens cloudy months out and moves the boundary to a later, clear month (`selection_status="quality_adjusted"` in the example below).
- The result: the row reports `trough_timing_status="point"` and a `trough_date` for the boundary month. But the month that stood out by a floor was the earlier, cloudy one. The guide says `trough_date` appears only when one month stood out, and here it was not that month.
- Example: `S|seasonal|240002` (`sine`, true trough February or March). January 2012 is 34.8% invalid and reads 0.51% extent, more than a floor (0.53) below every other month. The label says `point` at January. January is within a month of the truth. The boundary, and so `trough_date`, is May 2012 (1.11%, 9.8% invalid), two months from the nearer true month.
- On real records it was not seen. The current library on the three case-study catchments that route to per-year detection (Daly, Fitzroy WA and Gilbert, at 30, 60, 90 and 300 m) gives 85 `point` trough cycles and no mismatch. Those are large areas with clear dry seasons. Arm I's backgrounds are small real windows, where partly cloudy dry-season months are more common.

## What follows

**Fixed by the plans**
- L2 and R1 both fail, so neither makes a candidate. The library is unchanged.
- The two-sided trough label is off the `hydroseason-v0.3.0` candidate list.

**Done with these results (documentation only)**
- The guide and the hydrological-state reference cite L2's current-library run as a third known-truth test, and the guide's ranges now cover all three.

**Proposed. Each needs the project lead's decision.**
1. **Stop chasing a `point` fix through the label.** Three tests (G2, L1, L2) and two label changes show that `point` is not a reliable exact-date tier on weak or flat-bottomed seasons, and is no more reliable than `interval`. Further label variants would be fitting to these generators. Keep the guide's advice to read a single trough month as approximate.
2. **Relax the baseline's `point` requirement (B1, any dated status).** This is now the main open candidate for `hydroseason-v0.3.0`. L2 adds that `point` does not mark the more accurate boundaries. Measure its effect on real records and the case-study tables first.
3. **Fix the label/date mismatch (finding 2).** Two options, both method changes for `hydroseason-v0.3.0`:
   - judge the trough label on the same screened months the boundary uses;
   - or downgrade the label when the boundary is not in its own trough interval.

   First measure how often it happens on small real windows (the G1 windows). Until then, the docs could note that `trough_date` with `point` can follow a cloud-screened move.
4. **Close the rise question for now (clock-anchor thread).** On known truth, a standard 20% amplitude rise dates the transition better only on sharp-rise seasons, and worse on smooth ones. The trough-band end stays the anchor. Revisit only if real records with sharp rises become a target.

## Limits

- **Modelled noise and seasons.** Arm S noise is modelled, and arm I seasons are modelled. Arm I keeps real noise and real backgrounds, which carry their own seasons.
- **Two season shapes.** The `pulse` shape has a long flat low and a sharp rise. Real seasons vary.
- **Asymmetric truths.** The `sine` trough truth spans two months and the `sine` rise truth one, which favours the trough on `sine`. Scored against one true month only, the trough's lead in arm S becomes +0.03 (month m) or −0.09 (month m + 1), so arm S's rule 2 result depends on the truth definition. In arm I the trough leads under either month (+0.13 or +0.05). R1 fails in arm I either way.
- **L2 relabels after the library has run.** Trough refinement and pass 2 still saw the old label.
- **One rise rule, one fraction.** They were taken from the literature, not fitted.
- **The exploratory checks are rough.** The floor in finding 1 is derived from the reported ratio. Finding 2 uses invalid share from the record's counts.
- **Not accuracy for real places.**
