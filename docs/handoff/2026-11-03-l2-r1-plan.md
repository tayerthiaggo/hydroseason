# L2 and R1: a two-sided trough label, and the rise against the trough, on known truth (pre-registration)

Status: **frozen before any outcome**, 2026-11-03. The project lead asked for these two tests after L1 ([results](2026-11-03-l1-timing-labels-results.md)). The SHA-256 of this file goes into the receipt before the main runs. Later edits are reported as deviations.

The two tests share one corpus and one current-library run, but they answer separate questions and are decided separately.

## Background

L1 confirmed that `point` trough dates miss by more than a month far more often than 5%. It also found that widening the pass-1 tolerance does not fix this. Two findings from L1's exploratory checks lead here:

- **The trough label is one-sided.** A cycle runs to its own trough boundary, and the trough label searches from the last equivalent peak to that boundary (`assess_window_timing`, `trough_search="post_peak"`). Months after the boundary are never compared. In L1, a missed `point` trough usually had a month just after it that was as low, within one floor (56–75% of misses, against 20–51% of correct `point` dates).
- **On recession-shaped seasons the minimum is poorly placed, but the rise is sharp.** `pulse` troughs were dated early in 85–93% of misses. The library's own rise is the boundary plus one month, so it inherits that error. The clock-anchor stage 0 test listed as a limit that "a rise rule that does not depend on the trough band at all was not tested".

**L2** asks whether making the trough label two-sided makes `point` materially more reliable without losing records. **R1** asks whether a standard rise rule, independent of the trough, dates the dry-to-wet transition more accurately than the trough boundary does.

## Principles

- **Known answer.** Every record's trough month is set before any method sees it.
- **Not referenced to gauges.**
- **Fresh records.** No seed overlaps G2's or L1's.
- **Library untouched.** L2's change is made only inside worker processes, by wrapping `hydroseason.hydrological_state.detect_dynamic_hydrological_years`. The wrapper changes trough timing labels and nothing else. Routing and baseline eligibility then follow the changed labels through the library's own code. R1 is computed from the current library's outputs and the record's own values; it changes nothing.
- **Default is no change.** Neither test can change the library. A pass makes a candidate for a later method policy (`hydroseason-v0.3.0`) or evidence for the clock-anchor thread, and the project lead decides.

**Truth.**
- Trough, as in G2 and L1: `pulse` m; `sine` m or m + 1, measured to the nearer.
- Rise: the month the noise-free shape first exceeds 20% of its amplitude after the trough. That is m + 1 for `pulse` (shape 0.6) and m + 2 for `sine` (shape 0.25). The construction check confirms it before the freeze.
- Errors are measured to the nearest true month, and correct means within 1 month.

## L2: the two-sided trough label

**Setting `two_sided`.** For each cycle whose trough status is `point`, `interval` or `broad`:
1. Take the cycle's trough boundary B, its reported trough interval (start S, end E; S = E = B when the interval is missing) and its detectability floor F.
2. Let V be the lowest extent among the usable months from S to max(E, B).
3. Scan the months after B in order, up to but not including the next cycle's peak month (or to the end of the record):
   - an unusable month is skipped;
   - a usable month with extent ≤ V + F joins the equivalent set, and the scan goes on;
   - the first usable month above V + F ends the scan.
4. The new trough status is the library's own `_window_status` over S and the last month in the set, with broad allowed. The aggregate `timing_status` is recomputed with the library's `_aggregate_timing_status`. `trough_interval_end` becomes the last month in the set.

Usable means `candidate_usable` in the library's own `prepare_monthly_extent` output, with the run's config. The tolerance is the same one floor the label uses today. The rule can only widen a set, so it never promotes a status, and it never moves a boundary. `unresolved` and unlabelled cycles are unchanged.

**Decision rules.** L2 passes only if all of the following hold in both arms:

1. **`point` gets more reliable.** The share of `point` trough dates that are not correct, under `two_sided` minus under current, is at most −0.05. The upper end of its 95% interval is below 0 (2,000-draw bootstrap over records, seed 0).
2. **`point` is no less reliable than `interval`.** Under `two_sided`, the share of `point` dates that are not correct is at most the share of `interval` dates that are not correct.
3. **Records are not lost.** The share of seasonal records routed to per-year detection, under `two_sided` minus under current, is at least −0.02.
4. **Dates are unchanged.** In records routed to per-year detection under both, every trough boundary month is identical. This checks the implementation; any difference voids the run.

A rule whose case count is below 100 in an arm cannot be evaluated, and L2 then does not pass. If L2 passes, `two_sided` is a candidate for `hydroseason-v0.3.0`, after its effect on real records (the G1 windows) and on the case-study tables is measured, the docs are updated and the project lead decides.

**Reported, not used to decide:** the 5% safety bound of 0.2.0 (Wilson upper bound of the not-correct share of `point` dates); label shares by shape; accuracy by label; `point` misses by ratio band; baseline-eligible cycles, how many and how accurate; null records.

## R1: the rise against the trough

**The rise rule (fixed here).** It uses the amplitude-threshold start of season used in phenology (TIMESAT: Jönsson and Eklundh 2002, doi:10.1109/TGRS.2002.802519, and 2004, doi:10.1016/j.cageo.2004.05.006; review: Zeng et al. 2020, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2019.111511). It reads the library's peak month and the record's values, not the trough boundary or band.

For each cycle j with a peak month P:
1. Let A be the previous cycle's peak month. If the previous cycle has none, A is the month before the previous cycle's trough boundary, so that the window starts at that boundary.
2. Over the usable months after A up to P, let L be the lowest extent and H the extent at P.
3. If H ≤ L, the cycle has no rise. Otherwise the threshold is T = L + 0.2 × (H − L).
4. Walk back from the month before P. The first usable month with extent ≤ T is the last low month, and the rise R is the month after it. If there is none after A, the cycle has no rise.

The fraction 0.2 is fixed in advance. 0.1 and 0.5 are reported only.

**Pairs.** The rise of cycle j is compared with the trough boundary of cycle j − 1, which ends the same dry season. A pair is scored when cycle j − 1 has a trough boundary and cycle j has a peak month. A missing rise counts as not correct.

**Decision rules.** In each arm, on seasonal records routed to per-year detection under the current library:

1. **Recession seasons.** On `pulse` pairs, rise accuracy minus trough accuracy is at least +0.05, and the lower end of its 95% interval is above 0.
2. **Smooth seasons.** On `sine` pairs, rise accuracy minus trough accuracy is at least −0.02, and the lower end of its 95% interval is at least −0.05.
3. **Coverage.** At least 90% of pairs have a rise.

Intervals come from a 2,000-draw bootstrap over records (seed 0). A rule with fewer than 100 pairs cannot be evaluated. R1 passes only if all three hold in both arms.

**Reading, fixed in advance.** A pass means a rise rule independent of the trough dates the dry-to-wet transition better than the trough boundary does on known truth. That is evidence for a rise anchor (target T2) in the clock-anchor thread; the lead decides, and nothing changes in the library. A fail means this rise rule gives no accuracy gain over the trough boundary.

**Reported, not used to decide:** fractions 0.1 and 0.5, each scored against the rise its own rule gives on the noise-free shape (m + 2 and m + 4 for `sine`, m + 1 for `pulse`); accuracy and exact-month share by shape and ratio band; signed errors; the library's own rise (boundary plus one month) against the rise truth; results by peak timing status.

## Checks before the freeze (no accuracy computed on the main corpus)

1. **Construction.** G2's 24 noise-free records run under current and `two_sided`.
   - Interior trough boundaries must fall at the truth in at least 95% of cycles under both, as in L1.
   - `two_sided` must leave every `pulse` trough label unchanged. Changes on `sine` are recorded.
   - The rise rule must put at least 95% of interior rises at the rise truth for each shape. If it does not, R1 is dropped before the freeze, and that is recorded.
2. **Wrapper self-test.** Every worker checks the wrapper on two fixed cases. A `point` trough followed by a month within the floor must become `interval`. A `point` trough followed by a month above the floor must stay `point`. Each run records the result.
3. **Pilot.** The 100 pilot records run under both settings. Only run times and counts are looked at: `point` and `interval` trough dates under `two_sided`, and rise pairs by shape. If the pilot predicts fewer than 150 of any of these in either arm of the main corpus, that arm is scaled up before the freeze.

**Construction check outcome (recorded before the freeze).** Under both settings, all 216 interior trough boundaries fell at the truth for each shape. `two_sided` changed no trough label on these records, `pulse` or `sine`. Every wrapper self-test read back as expected, with the wrapper installed only under `two_sided`.

The first version of the rise rule put 209 of 216 interior rises at the truth for each shape. All 14 misses were one month late, in the cycle right after the record's first, peakless cycle. There the rule fell back to the previous trough boundary but left that boundary month out of the window, so it never saw the low. Step 1 of the rise rule was corrected to start the window at that boundary. The rerun put 216 of 216 rises at the truth for each shape, so R1 is kept. Only noise-free construction records had been scored when this was fixed.

**Pilot outcome (recorded before the freeze).** Scaled to the main corpus, the pilot predicts, under `two_sided`, about 1,930 `point` and 2,290 `interval` trough dates in arm S, and about 3,920 and 3,700 in arm I. It predicts about 3,400 rise pairs of each shape in arm S, and about 6,260 `sine` and 4,640 `pulse` pairs in arm I. Every count is far above the 150 required, so the sample sizes stand. Only counts and run times were looked at.

## Limits stated in advance

- **The noise and the seasons are modelled,** as in G2 and L1. Arm I keeps real noise.
- **The `pulse` shape is one choice,** with a long flat low and a sharp rise. Real recessions vary.
- **The truths are not symmetric.** The `sine` trough truth spans two months, and the `sine` rise truth one.
- **L2 changes the label after the library has run.** Inside the library, trough refinement and pass 2 still see the old label. Only routing, baseline eligibility and the reported labels follow the new one.
- **One rise rule and one fraction are tested.** They are not fitted.
- **Not accuracy for real places.**
